# Focused prior-art notes

These notes summarize the closest literature found during the AI-led search. They are not an exhaustive systematic review.

## Randić et al. (1993)

M. Randić, X. Guo, T. Oxley, H. Krishnapriyan, **"Wiener matrix: Source of novel graph invariants"**, *Journal of Chemical Information and Computer Sciences* 33(5), 709-716 (1993).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/ci00015a008

Why it matters: establishes Wiener-matrix/path machinery closely related to the local route count used in this project. This means the underlying `a*b` path weight should **not** be claimed as a new invention.

## Randić et al. (1994)

M. Randić, X. Guo, T. Oxley, H. Krishnapriyan, L. Naylor, **"Wiener Matrix Invariants"**, *Journal of Chemical Information and Computer Sciences* 34(2), 361-367 (1994).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/ci00018a022

Why it matters: develops additional invariants extracted from the Wiener matrix.

## Diudea (1996)

M. V. Diudea, **"Walk Numbers eW_M: Wiener-Type Numbers of Higher Rank"**, *Journal of Chemical Information and Computer Sciences* 36(3), 535-540 (1996).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/ci950134+

Why it matters: shows that higher-rank/matrix transformations of Wiener-type data were already being studied. The operations described there are matrix/walk constructions, not the fixed-distance entrywise-square statistic used here.

## Gutman et al. (1997)

I. Gutman, W. Linert, I. Lukovits, A. A. Dobrynin, **"Trees with Extremal Hyper-Wiener Index: Mathematical Basis and Chemical Applications"**, *Journal of Chemical Information and Computer Sciences* 37(2), 349-354 (1997).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/ci960139m

Why it matters: path/star extremality for Wiener-related tree indices is established prior art. Therefore the broad statement "the path is extremal for a Wiener-type quantity" is not new by itself.

## Guo et al. (2006)

X. Guo, D. J. Klein, W. Yan, Y.-N. Yeh, **"Hyper-Wiener vector, Wiener matrix sequence, and Wiener polynomial sequence of a graph"**, *International Journal of Quantum Chemistry* 106(8), 1756-1761 (2006).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/qua.20958

Why it matters: explicitly organizes higher Wiener information by distance layer. This is the closest conceptual predecessor found to the project's fixed-`k` treatment. The searched description uses first powers/sums of layer entries rather than the squared layer sum studied here.

## Golumbic and Jamison (1985)

M. C. Golumbic, R. E. Jamison, **"The edge intersection graphs of paths in a tree"**, *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* 38(1), 8-22 (1985).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(85)90088-7

Why it matters: path intersections in trees are a well-established subject. This paper studies the graph class generated by edge intersections of path families, not the random common-path-length distribution studied in the present note.

## Current novelty position

The focused search did not find an explicit prior treatment of all three of the following together:

1. the fixed-distance quadratic quantity `sum_Q c(Q)^2` over all `k`-edge tree subpaths;
2. its exact interpretation as `E[(L-k+1)_+]` for two independent uniformly random routes; and
3. the first-order stochastic extremal theorem `L_T <=_st L_{P_n}` for every `n`-vertex tree.

This absence is only evidence, not proof, of novelty. A serious novelty check should next use MathSciNet, zbMATH, Web of Science/Scopus, and citation chasing in older chemical graph theory.
